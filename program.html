Showcase Concert with The Klezmatics

The Klezmatics are one of the most famous klezmer bands of all time, justifiably so, and the only winners of a Grammy award. The band was crucial to the “klezmer revival” that emerged in the 1970s and 1980s. They learned deeply the traditional language of klezmer from the old days, immersing themselves in recordings from the early 20th century, and blended that traditional style with modern sounds like rock and blues. Their arrangements keep the listener on the edge of the seat, and their song interpretations express a deep humanity and sense of purpose. They have recorded extensively, and the Klez Fest Midwest Co-Directors have listened to those recordings obsessively. At 40 years old, they are still performing all over the world and still recording. This is a once in a lifetime opportunity to hear the band free of charge in a great concert hall.

Dan’s World Music Fest

Come hear three live world music bands and dance your heart out at Dan’s World Music Fest on Saturday, October 24 at the East Side Club in Madison. As a World Music DJ on WORT for over 30 years, the late Dan Talmo always said, Whenever you can, listen to WORT, and also go out and hear live music! Here’s your chance to tribute Dan, give to WORT, and tap your feet to great world music. This event is a WORT fundraiser being held in collaboration with Klez Fest Midwest. First, you’ll hear Szászka, specializing in Hungarian and Transylvanian dance house music. Next, the LuLu Quintet, will play the sparkling French/Romany “hot club” jazz inspired by Django Reinhardt, followed by Yid Vicious’s sophisticated yet whimsical version of Klezmer and Yiddish folk and dance music.

Nigunim Trio

Nigunim and zmiros are songs of devotion and praise, spiritual Hassidic-Jewish chants whose purpose is to induce a state of piety and ecstasy in both performer and listener. The Nigunim Trio—Lorin Sklamberg (accordion) and Frank London (trumpet), co-founders of the Klezmatics, and pianist Rob Schwimmer—perform original and traditional nigunim and zmiros drawn from the Belzer, Gerer, Lubavitcher, and Skulaner dynasties of the Hassidim, performed with a uniquely styled musical and spiritual imprint.
